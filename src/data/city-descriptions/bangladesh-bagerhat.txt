Located in southwestern Bangladesh, Bagerhat is a historic and culturally rich city, situated on the banks of the Baleshwar River. As one of the country's oldest cities, Bagerhat is home to a significant population of approximately 140,000 residents, with a predominantly Muslim community. The city is often referred to as the "city of sixty domes" due to its 60 unique mosques built in the 15th century, showcasing the country's rich Islamic heritage and architectural legacy. The strong influence of Islam on the local culture and way of life is evident in the city's numerous mosques, Islamic festivals, and traditional clothing.

Muslims make up the overwhelming majority of the population, and the community is tightly-knit, with many residents actively participating in prayer times, charity, and social activities. The city's Islamic culture revolves around the five daily prayer times, with mosques serving as the focal points for community gatherings and events. Despite the country's predominantly Muslim population, Islamic life in Bagerhat is characterized by a strong sense of community, and the city's residents come together regularly to observe Islamic traditions and engage in charitable activities.

Bagerhat has a subtropical climate, with the city experiencing a hot summer season that lasts from March to May. The best time to visit the city is from October to March, when the temperatures are cooler and more pleasant. During this period, the city's residents gather for daily prayers, often accompanied by the chanting of the Quran, further adding to the city's serene ambiance. The winter season offers the perfect opportunity for visitors to explore the city's historical sites, experience the local culture, and immerse themselves in the city's prayer times and Islamic traditions.
