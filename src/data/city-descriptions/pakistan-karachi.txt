Located in the southeastern part of Pakistan, Karachi boasts a significant position as the country's largest metropolitan city. With a massive population of over 14 million residents, it serves as a vital economic and cultural hub for the region. The city holds immense historical and cultural significance, with various archaeological and Islamic landmarks such as the Quaid-e-Millath Government Hospital's mosque, testifying to its rich past.

The Muslim community plays an integral part in the city's culture and way of life. With a substantial population of Muslims, the city is home to numerous mosques such as the Quaid-e-Azam Mosque, which holds great importance for the locals. The city showcases the traditional Islamic values, blending them seamlessly with the urban setting. Locals gather at different mosques at set prayer times to perform their daily prayers, adhering to the Islamic calendar and the lunar cycle. As the city's Islamic roots and traditions remain strong, it presents a fascinating blend of faith and modernity.

Karachi's climate is characterized by high temperatures during the summer months, especially from May to September, with the peak temperature often reaching 46°C. The best time to visit the city is from October to March, when the weather is milder and more suitable for exploration. During these cooler months, the residents can perform their morning prayers without facing extreme temperature conditions, offering a more comfortable and serene experience for those seeking their daily spiritual solace. The gentle weather provides a perfect opportunity for the residents and tourists alike to engage in their daily routines.
