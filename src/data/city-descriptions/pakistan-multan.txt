Located in the southern part of Punjab province, Multan is one of the oldest continually inhabited cities in the Indian subcontinent, with a rich history dating back over 4,000 years. The city boasts a population of approximately 1.7 million, with a significant majority belonging to the Muslim community. As a place of great importance for both Sunni and Shia Muslims, Multan is steeped in Islamic heritage and is home to over 400 historic mosques, including the majestic Wazir Khan Mosque, a renowned example of Mughal architecture.

The Muslim community in Multan plays a vital role in preserving Islamic tradition and culture. The city is home to several prominent Islamic institutions, including the renowned Bahawal Haq Khiyali Madrasa, which attracts students from across the country. The city's Muslim population actively participates in various religious events and festivals, such as Eid-al-Fitr and Eid-al-Adha, and the community comes together to observe prayer times, ensuring that daily prayers are performed in accordance with the Quran.

The city's climate is typically hot and dry, with temperatures often soaring above 40 degrees Celsius during the summer months. The best time to visit Multan is from September to November or from March to April, when the weather is relatively more bearable. During these periods, the city's residents and visitors alike can comfortably perform their daily prayers outdoors, under the pleasant weather conditions. As Multan remains an essential destination for Muslims seeking spiritual growth and Islamic knowledge, the community continues to thrive, with prayer times playing a central role in the daily lives of its residents.
